WHY THIS AMENDMENT
  All earlier results are known (Amendments 2 to 11, post hoc analyses PH1 to PH4, Error Logs 1 to 6).
  Four critiques of the manuscript obtained before resubmission (13 November 2026; one written by
  the AI assistant used for the analyses, three supplied by the author; none is a journal peer
  review) raised the following points, which these analyses address:
  (1) a test of target-weighted kappa outside the population whose prevalence it uses; (2) its
  sensitivity to a wrong estimate of the target prevalence; (3) the Ki-67 implication in percentage
  points; (4) goodness of fit of the binormal model, including the equal-variance assumption;
  (5) the dependence of the case-mix share of variance on the range of imposed shares; (6) the
  stability of the minimum-misclassification Ki-67 optimum; (7) cohort characteristics (REMARK 13).
  These analyses were conceived after all earlier results and the critiques were known; registration
  fixes their specification before their own results are seen.

ANALYSES
  N2  Transfer between natural subgroups (SCAN-B; P2 primary, P1 secondary). Three splits of the
      luminal tumors, fixed now: tumor size <= 20 mm versus > 20 mm; node-negative (NodeNegative)
      versus node-positive (1to3 or 4toX; SubMicroMet and missing excluded); age < 50 versus >= 50
      years. Each split is used in both directions: derive in one subgroup, apply in the other.
      Derivation: the whole subgroup, and 500 samples of min(400, subgroup size) tumors drawn with
      replacement from the subgroup (natural case mix and spectrum; no resampling within subtype).
      Rules: K, ACC, PM, YJ; SK_oracle (weighted to the target subgroup's luminal B share);
      SK_population (weighted to the luminal B share of all SCAN-B luminal tumors, a stand-in for an
      estimate from a population-based series of the same region).
      Outcomes in the target subgroup: RMSE and bias of q_A (target luminal A distribution) against
      (i) the same rule applied to the whole target subgroup and (ii) the K optimum of the whole
      target subgroup; percentage of target luminal tumors labeled differently from the K optimum;
      mean kappa against PAM50; the best attainable kappa.
      Primary split, fixed rule: the split with the largest absolute difference in luminal B share
      between its two subgroups (P2).
  N3  Misspecified target share. Design of Amendment 11 O3 (within-subtype resampling, n = 400,
      imposed shares 25% to 65%, 500 samples), P2, three cohorts. SK weighted to the true target
      share plus delta, delta in {-15, -10, -5, 0, +5, +10, +15} points (bounded to 5% to 95%), on
      the same samples as K. Outcomes: RMSE and bias of q_A against the full-cohort K optimum,
      percentage relabeled, mean kappa.
  N4  Ki-67 prediction in percentage points. Binormal fit to consensus Ki-67 (same method as
      Amendment 11 O5). Model q_A of K, ACC, PM and YJ at luminal B shares of 25% and 65%,
      converted to Ki-67 percentage points through the luminal A distribution (linear interpolation
      between order statistics); difference between the two shares. 95% interval from 1,000
      bootstrap samples drawn within subtype, refitting the model each time.
  N5  Binormal goodness of fit, each RNA cohort and score and Ki-67: a, b, R^2 of the probit-probit
      fit and number of points in the fitting window; 95% bootstrap interval of b (500 samples
      within subtype; RNA only); slopes of the K and ACC model optima with b fixed at 1 (equal
      variance), next to the free-b slopes and the Amendment 11 resampled slopes.
  N7  Case-mix share of variance (Amendment 11 O2 definition) recomputed from the deposited
      Amendment 11 derivations over subsets of imposed shares: {25..65} (registered), {35, 45, 55},
      {25, 35, 45} and {45, 55, 65}; RNA and Ki-67.
  N8  Ki-67 near-optimal region. In the full GSE81538 luminal set weighted to luminal B shares of
      15% to 65%: the accuracy and kappa of every candidate threshold, the maximum, and the lowest
      and highest thresholds within 0.01 of the maximum.
  N9  Characteristics of each cohort (age, size, node status, grade, stage, chemotherapy where
      recorded). No outcome or subtype comparison.

INTERPRETATION RULES FIXED NOW
  - N2: target-weighted kappa is reported as transferring better between natural subgroups if, in
    the primary split (P2), its RMSE against the target K optimum is lower than that of K in both
    directions; stated separately for SK_oracle and SK_population. If the primary split's subgroups
    differ by less than 5 points in luminal B share, N2 is reported as uninformative about the
    adjustment. All splits are reported whatever they show.
  - N3: the tolerance is the largest |delta| such that, for every misspecification up to that size
    in both directions, SK has a lower RMSE than K in all three cohorts.
  - N4 is a model prediction, not an observed movement; it is reported with its interval and the
    number of points behind the fit. If fewer than 3 points lie in the fitting window, N4 is not
    reported. No statement on Ki-67 is called confirmed.
  - N5: the equal-variance assumption is described as adequate for a cohort and score if the 95%
    interval of b includes 1. No pass or fail criterion for the model as a whole.
  - N8: the movement of the Ki-67 accuracy optimum between 25% and 65% luminal B is described as
    robust only if the near-optimal regions at those two shares do not overlap.
  - N7 and N9 are descriptive. No adjustment for multiple comparisons.
  - All results enter the manuscript or Online Resource 4 whatever they show.
